# Lead: Build the per-state conversion map for `/execute` — which koto state gets which `default_action`, what each conversion depends on, and how risky it is.

## Findings

Scope: `skills/execute/koto-templates/execute.md` (frontmatter states + prose body, full read), `skills/execute/SKILL.md` (section map re-confirmed against round 1's line citations), `skills/execute/references/cross-issue-context.md` (full read, 15 lines). Base data is round 1's inventory (`wip/research/explore_koto-runs-commands_r1_lead-execute-inventory.md`), re-walked state by state.

One scoping note up front: `/execute`'s koto template declares **12 states**. Of the 53 agent-instructed commands round 1 counted across all three files, **40 live inside the template body** (execute.md, attributable to a specific state) and **2 more fire from `cross-issue-context.md`** inside `spawn_and_await`'s loop — so 42 of 53 are state-scoped and in reach of a per-state `default_action`. The remaining **11 live in SKILL.md**: preflight checks, the coordinated-path `shirabe validate` calls, the resume-ladder lookup. Those execute above and around the koto state machine — in the parent skill's own phase logic, not inside any `koto next` call — so no per-state `default_action` can reach them regardless of design. They are out of scope for this map by construction, not by risk judgment; I call this out again in the quantification section.

**Revision, folded in after the table below was first drafted:** the orchestrator's empirical probe (`wip/research/explore_koto-runs-commands_r2_lead-empirical-probe.md`) found that a `default_action` cannot invoke *any* koto subcommand that touches the session store — writes, reads, even `koto workflows` and `koto status` — without deadlocking until the engine's 30-second timeout kills it. The outer `koto next` call holds a workspace-scoped lock across its whole advance loop, and a nested koto call waits on that same lock. This is not a capability gap `needs-output-routing` can describe — a future output-routing feature built *inside the engine* could still route data without a nested CLI call, but any block whose command chain includes a literal `koto context add`/`get`/`workflows`/`status` invocation is **structurally excluded**, not merely dependency-blocked. Per the orchestrator's framing, I've marked those rows accordingly below rather than filing them under Wave B: they are not waiting on a koto feature that's on someone's list, they're permanently agent-issued given how the lock works today. This changes the shape of the map substantially — most of what I'd scoped as Wave A/B in `spawn_and_await` and parts of `orchestrator_setup`, `escalate`, and `done_blocked` turns out to be Wave C. The table and wave sections below are updated to reflect this; the run-cascade.sh-based rows (`plan_completion`) are unaffected — the orchestrator separately confirmed that script makes no koto calls internally.

### Quantification against the 42%-elimination claim

The koto-lock finding changes the answer from "close to the claim" to "well short of it." Wave A is **8 of 53 ≈ 15%**. Wave A + Wave B together is **14-15 of 53 ≈ 26-28%**, still well under the claimed ~42% even assuming every open engine capability (output-routing both directions) gets built. The 25 commands in the structurally-excluded bucket are not a "later" wave — per the orchestrator's framing they're permanently agent-issued given how the session-store lock works today, so they cannot be counted toward the claim under any read of "convertible."

**For `/execute` specifically, the 42% claim does not hold**, not even as an aspirational ceiling contingent on future koto work. Its practical ceiling, combining the lock exclusion with the 11 SKILL.md commands that were already out of reach: (53 − 25 − 11) = 17 commands are ever reachable by a per-state `default_action`, i.e. **32% is the absolute ceiling for `/execute`**, and only 26-28 points of that is achievable without deeper engine surgery than output-routing (a change to the lock's scope, which the orchestrator's probe flags as a separate, larger question). If the 42% figure was derived from a different template or from `/work-on`, it may hold there — the orchestrator's write-up notes `/work-on`'s eight retry-clearing blocks were also assumed convertible and are now confirmed dead for the same lock reason, which suggests the same ceiling problem generalizes rather than being unique to `/execute`.

### Duplication flagged (lead's ask #3)

Confirmed and detailed above: `ci_monitor`'s prose-restated `gh pr view --json mergeStateStatus --jq .mergeStateStatus` (execute.md:579) is near-identical to the `merge_state_clean` gate's own command (execute.md:244), which koto already runs and already exposes as `gates.merge_state_clean.exit_code` — visible in the `ci_monitor` state's own `when` clauses (execute.md:257-258). This is the one state in the map where "should this get a `default_action`" is the wrong question; the redundant call should be deleted, not automated.

### `PLAN_SLUG` derivation (lead's ask #4)

The five sites (execute.md:393, 440, 484, 530, 546) all re-run `basename {{PLAN_DOC}} .md | sed 's/^PLAN-//'` even though `PLAN_SLUG` is already declared as a required template variable (frontmatter, execute.md:14-22) specifically — per the frontmatter's own comment — because the `worktree_discipline_check` gate needs a compile-time-validated `{{KEY}}` reference rather than shell-side derivation. Every one of the five body sites should simply reference `{{PLAN_SLUG}}` directly instead of re-deriving it; this is not a `default_action` conversion at all, it's deleting five redundant one-liners in favor of the variable the template already declares and validates. It's the single highest-leverage, lowest-risk cleanup in this map — no koto changes, no behavior change, and it removes literal duplicated shell from five separate agent-facing sites in one pass.

## Implications

The map now splits along two independent axes instead of one. The first axis is the one I originally drew: states whose command work is *already* deterministic given the stated decision rule (`orchestrator_setup`'s create-vs-override check, `spawn_and_await`'s `OUTCOME=` rule, `plan_completion`'s cascade) versus states whose work is genuinely interpretive (`worktree_discipline_check`'s classification, `pr_finalization`'s Part 1 prose) — the latter don't convert regardless of what koto adds, because the decision rule itself isn't statable as shell. That axis is unchanged by the lock finding.

The second axis, added by the orchestrator's probe, cuts across the first and turns out to dominate the quantification: whether the block's *data source* is a live koto call. `spawn_and_await`'s `OUTCOME=` rule is a clean example of a command that's deterministic on the first axis (no interpretation needed) but structurally excluded on the second (the data can only be fetched via `koto workflows`, which deadlocks inside an action) — so it converts on neither axis, for two unrelated reasons that happen to coincide. `default_action` design needs to treat these as three buckets, not two: mechanical-and-koto-free (converts now), mechanical-but-koto-sourced (blocked on the lock, not on output-routing), and genuinely interpretive (never converts). Conflating the second and third buckets — as I initially did before this update — understates how much of the "mechanical" work is actually reachable, because a large fraction of what's mechanical in this template exists specifically to read from or write to koto's own context store, which is exactly the operation the lock forbids from inside an action.

## Surprises

1. `spawn_and_await`'s `OUTCOME=` line was the single JUDGMENT-classified line in round 1 that, on closer read, isn't judgment at all — the decision rule stated in prose ("`all_success` if all children reached a non-failure terminal state, else `needs_attention`") is a pure function of data koto already tracks per child. It's classified JUDGMENT today only because the agent has to fetch that data by eyeballing `koto workflows` output rather than because interpreting it requires reasoning. But the fetch itself (`koto workflows`) is a live koto call, and the orchestrator's probe confirmed those deadlock inside an action — so this line was never going to convert either way, for two independent reasons that happen to land on the same line. It's a useful cautionary example: "the decision rule is mechanical" is necessary but not sufficient for `default_action` eligibility.

2. `plan_completion` is the template's own best argument for what a safe `default_action` looks like, and it already exists, confirmed doubly now: `run-cascade.sh` has self-detecting idempotency (a pre-probe that distinguishes "already terminal" from "needs the cascade"), an internal `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` guard that `orchestrator_setup` conspicuously lacks, and — per the orchestrator's direct check — no koto calls inside it at all, so it's immune to the lock finding that gutted most of the rest of this map. It's the one state where "convert the whole thing" survives both axes.

3. The lock finding reshapes the map far more than any individual state's classification did. Before it, I'd estimated `/execute`'s Wave A at 19-20 commands (~36-38%); after it, Wave A is 8 (~15%). The commands that moved are overwhelmingly the ones whose entire purpose is talking to koto's own context store — `spawn_and_await`'s settled-branch reads, the cross-issue-context loop, the `batch_final_view`/`failure_reason` fetches. That's not a coincidence: those are exactly the commands round 1 flagged as KOTO-PROTOCOL (14 of the 53), plus the settled-branch portion of orchestrator_setup's MECHANICAL rows. The KOTO-PROTOCOL bucket, which round 1 treated as agent-to-engine plumbing distinct from the MECHANICAL bucket's conversion candidacy, turns out to be *structurally* excluded from conversion for a reason neither round 1 nor my first pass had visibility into.

## Open Questions

- Whether a `default_action` can consume evidence the agent just submitted in the same `koto next` call (needed for `pr_finalization`'s apply step to fire after Part 1 prose exists) is a distinct capability from output-routing *into* evidence, and doesn't fit cleanly into the four dependency categories this map used. It's also unaffected by the lock finding, since consuming already-submitted evidence isn't a nested koto call — worth a fifth category, or worth folding into `needs-output-routing` explicitly as "bidirectional," so other leads don't end up scoping only the read-out-of-action-output half.
- The orchestrator's own open question — where exactly the lock is taken and whether its workspace-wide, whole-advance-loop scope is deliberate — is the single highest-leverage thing that could reopen this map. If the lock could be scoped narrower (e.g., released between states in a chain, or exempted for read-only session queries), a large share of the 25-command structurally-excluded bucket could move back into Wave B. Worth asking whoever owns engine locking before treating today's ceiling as final.
- Should `orchestrator_setup` get a `git rev-parse --show-toplevel`-style guard modeled on `run-cascade.sh`'s, independent of the `default_action` question — it's the state with the highest writes-remote risk and currently the weakest wrong-directory protection in the template, and unlike everything else in this map, hardening it doesn't depend on any koto change at all.

## Summary

The koto-lock finding — a `default_action` cannot call any koto subcommand that touches the session store without deadlocking — cuts this map's convertible surface roughly in half from my first pass.
